Rust on the path of the Beams: Old Gods whisper in the West

By Cuthbert Allgood, Staff Writer

Travelers returning from the western Barony of Farson report unsettling changes in the landscape. Crops fail under a blood-red sun, machines long dead have begun to hum with a cold, unnatural life, and one farmer claims to have seen a Great Old One slithering along the edge of the Send River, whispering in the Tongue of the Prim.​
​
Experts from the Vannay School suspect a weakening in the Beam — possibly the Bear Beam, once guarded by the great cybertic guardian Shardik. Rumors persist that Shardik's circuits have gone silent, his presence now no more than a scorched ring in the forest.​​
​
Mid-worlders are urged to avoid travel along the western trail until the gunslingers investigate. In the words of the old rhyme: "When Beam breaks, the world shakes."​​
​
Let all remember the face of their fathers, and may the Tower still stand.

Roland sigthed near Calla Bryn Sturgis: Ka turns again

By Cuthbert Allgood, Staff Writer

Eyewitnesses from the Calla report sightings of a tall man in a long duster, two sandalwood guns on his hips, passing like a ghost through the rice fields at dawn. Some say it is Roland Deschain of Gilead — last of the line of Eld — who once defended the Calla from the Wolves and crossed into Thunderclap with his ka-tet.​​
​
One witness, Tian Jaffords’ youngest, claimed the gunslinger spoke softly to a rose blooming from cracked earth near the edge of town. Others saw him walking the path toward the Dogan ruins, head bowed.​​
​
Whether this is truly Roland or only a shade remains unclear, but in Mid-World, time is a wheel, and Ka is always spinning.​​
​
Be it dream, memory, or fate: the Tower calls, and he follows.​

Blaine the mono ratlles to life: passengers warned to stay clear

By Cuthbert Allgood, Staff Writer

Last week, an echo of steel and circuitry rang out from the ruins of Lud. Blaine the Mono, once thought lost to madness and time, has reportedly powered up his monorail line. The ancient AI, last encountered on his fatal route to Topeka, is rumored to be reassembling his consciousness, pulling whispers of riddles from the air.​
​
A group of brave scavengers who ventured near the Cradle of Blaine described rhythmic pulses and the ghost of a laugh that made their blood run cold. “It was like hearing your own thoughts scream,” said one, still trembling.​
​
The Guardians of the Beam advise all readers to steer well clear of the Barony of Lud and surrounding wastelands. As always, remember: “Blaine is a pain, and that’s the truth.”
